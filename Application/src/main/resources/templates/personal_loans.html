Charges You Must Know

The interest rate headline is just the beginning. Read the fine print on these five charges.

Processing Fee
Banks deduct a processing fee of 1% – 2.5% of the loan amount + 18% GST before crediting the funds. On a ₹10 Lakh loan, this could be ₹10,000–25,000 deducted upfront. Always check whether the fee is deducted from disbursal or collected separately, and factor this into your actual cost of borrowing.
Foreclosure / Prepayment Penalty
If you clear the loan early, banks charge a foreclosure penalty of 2% – 4% on the outstanding principal. Some banks allow prepayment only after 12 EMIs. RBI has mandated no foreclosure charges for floating-rate loans — but personal loans are fixed-rate, so this exemption does not apply.
EMI Bounce / Late Payment Charges
A returned EMI (insufficient funds) attracts a bounce charge of ₹500 – ₹1,000 per instance, plus penal interest on the overdue EMI at 2% per month. Even a single bounce gets reported to CIBIL, immediately impacting your credit score. Always keep 2 months' EMI as a buffer in your linked account.
Flat Rate vs. Reducing Balance Trap
Some lenders (DSAs and NBFCs especially) quote a "Flat Rate" which sounds lower but is costlier. A Flat Rate of 10% on ₹5L for 3 years = ₹1.5L interest, while a Reducing Balance rate of 10% on the same = ₹83,000. The flat-rate equivalent APR is roughly 1.8× the stated rate. Always confirm which method the lender uses.
Loan Insurance (Optional but Pushed)
Banks often bundle a credit life insurance policy with personal loans, adding ₹5,000–40,000 to the loan principal. While coverage is useful, it is legally optional — you cannot be denied a loan for refusing insurance. The premium is typically added to your loan amount, meaning you pay interest on the insurance cost too.

Key Concept

Reducing Balance vs. Flat Rate — The ₹50,000 Difference

The single most important thing to check before signing your loan agreement. Most banks use reducing balance — but always verify, because the difference is massive.

Flat Rate Quoted
Lender says "10% p.a. flat" — sounds low. Interest is calculated on the original principal every month
True Cost = 18%+
A 10% flat rate on ₹5L for 3 years = ₹1.5L interest. The effective APR is ~18%, not 10%
Reducing Balance
Interest is charged only on the outstanding principal — so it drops every month as you repay
Always Confirm
Ask: "Is this reducing balance or flat rate?" before signing. Fake Bank uses reducing balance only
Decision Guide
Borrow Smart — 3 Rules

A personal loan is a powerful tool — but only if you use it right. These three principles keep you in control.

01
Keep EMI Below 40% of Income

The golden rule of borrowing. If your total EMIs (all loans combined) exceed 40% of your take-home salary, you're over-leveraged. Lenders use this ratio (FOIR) and may reject your application too.

02
Don't Borrow More Than You Need

Pre-approved offers may tempt you with higher amounts. Borrow only what you'll use — the processing fee, interest, and potential prepayment penalty make every unused rupee costly.

03
Compare APR, Not Just the Rate

The Annual Percentage Rate (APR) includes the interest rate plus all fees. Two loans at 12% interest can have APRs of 13.8% and 15.5% depending on processing fees. Always compare APR.
